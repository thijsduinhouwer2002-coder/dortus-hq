## Tekst
```
Click Joints Pro turns a rigged character into printable parts that move.

Pick your rigged figure and press one button. Figure from rig reads the bones you made: every elbow and knee gets a hinge with an end stop, and the shoulders, hips and neck get a ball joint, with the clearance you set in millimetres. If a bone has a Limit Rotation constraint, the hinge stops at that angle (30° to 150°). No limit on one side? It takes the other side. No limit at all? A preset per body part. Next to each joint you see how far it bends, in degrees, before you print.

- Every range in one view: a degree arc at each joint, and a list in the panel with the name, type, range and where the range came from (from rig, mirror or preset)
- Recognises Rigify, Mixamo, Auto-Rig Pro, Unreal, Character Creator, VRoid, 3ds Max Biped and Daz bone names
- No rig, or already split your model with another tool? Put the 3D cursor on a knee or elbow and pick one of six body-part presets
- Fit test strip: five joints from 0.10 to 0.30 mm clearance, each with its number, so you know which gap fits your printer
- Red where parts hit past the stop, a green fit band, export to STL or 3MF
- A joint that doesn't fit is skipped, and the list tells you why

Print the Fit test strip first: every printer is different. Works best on figures about 200 mm tall or more with separate arms and legs. Best in Blender 4.5 or newer; in 4.2 it works, but some joints may need to be placed by hand.

Reply here or mail thijs@dortus.com and tell us what you'd like to print. If you write about it somewhere else, please mention you got it for free.

Questions and bug reports welcome in this thread. We'll post updates here too.

Het antwoord met de gratis add-on komt dan niet meer in dat draadje; wie de route wil, vindt hem in ons release-draadje.
```
For a 114 mm figure we'd model it as one closed mesh first (head, torso, arms, legs, no joints yet), then cut the joints in afterwards. Getting the proportions right is the hard part; the joints are easier to add once the shape is there.

A few things that help at that size:
- Keep the limbs roughly 10 mm thick or more where a ball joint goes, or the wall around the socket gets too thin to print.
- Leave about 0.2 mm gap between ball and socket to start with, and print one small test joint before the whole figure. Every printer and filament is a bit different.
- Split the figure so each part prints without heavy supports inside the socket.

Happy to take a look once you have a first blockout.
```
