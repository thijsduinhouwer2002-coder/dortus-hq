**Tekst:**
```
Hi Hackaday,

A tip about a tool we made, in case it is interesting for the 3D printing crowd.

Click Joints is a free, open source (GPL) Blender add-on for making poseable printed figures. You put the 3D cursor where the figure should bend, click Place joint, and it cuts the mesh into two watertight parts with a ball and socket or a pin and hole, sized with the clearance your printer needs, in millimetres (0.1 to 0.3 mm is typical).

The part we think is fun: for a ball joint it then measures how far the joint can bend before the parts collide, in 8 directions, and shows that range in the viewport, for example "Bends ±20°". Tilt the part past that limit and the spot where it hits turns red. A ring on the cut shows the fit from the wall thickness: green is good, yellow is tight ("Tight: wall 0.89 mm, aim for 1.5 mm or more"), red is too thin or breaks through the surface. A spot that is too thin for a joint is refused with a message instead of giving you a broken part. Export is STL or 3MF, one file per part.

Free on Blender Extensions: https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/click-joints/
Code: https://github.com/thijsduinhouwer2002-coder/click-joints

There is also a paid version, Click Joints Pro ($19, on sale this week), that reads a rigged character and puts a hinge with an end stop at every elbow and knee and a ball joint at shoulders, hips and neck. The end stop comes from the Limit Rotation constraints in the rig. It works best on figures about 200 mm tall or more with separate arms and legs, in Blender 4.5 or newer. Walkthrough video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4-3l3MZZWPc
More: https://dortus.com/click-joints/?van=hackaday

The fit and bend range are measured in Blender. Every printer is different, so we tell users to print one test joint first to find the right clearance for their printer.

Attached: a GIF of the free version placing a ball joint on a wooden figure, and a GIF of a Pro hinge bending through its range (0 to 115 degrees).

Thijs
Dortus
```
